# Study Roadmap: Data Science and Data Analyst

Two separate books, each going from foundations to advanced topics. Rename or move chapters to match the courses actually taken.

---

# Book 1: Data Science

## Part I: Foundations (Year 1)
1. Mathematics: linear algebra, calculus, optimization basics
2. Probability: random variables, distributions, limit theorems
3. Descriptive and inferential statistics: estimation, confidence intervals, hypothesis tests
4. Programming basics: Python, R, algorithms and data structures
5. Databases and SQL fundamentals
6. Tools: Git, Jupyter, command line

## Part II: Core Statistical Modeling (Year 1 to 2)
7. Linear regression and diagnostics
8. Generalized linear models (logistic, Poisson, Gamma)
9. Multivariate analysis: PCA, factor analysis, clustering
10. Time series: ARIMA, seasonality, forecasting
11. Survival analysis and duration models
12. Bayesian statistics and simulation (Monte Carlo, MCMC)

## Part III: Machine Learning (Year 2)
13. ML workflow: train/test split, cross-validation, bias-variance
14. Supervised learning: trees, random forests, gradient boosting (XGBoost, LightGBM)
15. Unsupervised learning and dimensionality reduction
16. Feature engineering and handling imbalanced data
17. Model evaluation, calibration, interpretability (SHAP, LIME)

## Part IV: Advanced and Modern Topics (Year 2 to 3)
18. Deep learning: neural networks, CNNs, RNNs/LSTMs
19. NLP and transformers/LLMs
20. Big data and distributed computing (Spark)
21. MLOps: pipelines, deployment, monitoring, automation
22. Data engineering: ETL, data quality, reconciliation

## Part V: Specialization in Risk and Insurance (Year 3)
23. Credit risk and scoring models
24. Actuarial modeling: frequency/severity, pricing, reserving
25. Risk measures: VaR, expected shortfall, stress testing
26. Regulatory context (Basel, Solvency, Takaful principles)

## Part VI: Applied Work
27. Capstone project: health insurance actuarial pricing (PFE)
28. Internship: data engineering and automation at the bank
29. Internship: data reconciliation at the telecom company
30. Personal projects, competitions, publications

# Book 2: Data Analyst

## Part I: Foundations (Year 1)
1. Statistics essentials: descriptive stats, distributions, sampling
2. Excel: formulas, pivot tables, Power Query
3. SQL: queries, joins, window functions, CTEs
4. Python/R for analysis: pandas, tidyverse
5. Data types, data collection, and survey design

## Part II: Data Preparation (Year 1 to 2)
6. Data cleaning: missing values, outliers, duplicates, inconsistencies
7. Data transformation and merging
8. Data quality checks and reconciliation
9. Documentation and data dictionaries

## Part III: Analysis (Year 2)
10. Exploratory data analysis
11. Hypothesis testing, A/B testing, correlation analysis
12. Regression for business questions
13. Segmentation and cohort analysis
14. Time series and trend analysis
15. KPI design and metrics frameworks

## Part IV: Visualization and Communication (Year 2 to 3)
16. Visualization principles and chart selection
17. Dashboards: Power BI, Tableau
18. Python visualization: matplotlib, seaborn, plotly
19. Data storytelling and report writing
20. Presenting to non-technical stakeholders

## Part V: Advanced Analytics and Automation (Year 3)
21. Data warehouses and data modeling (star schema)
22. ETL and workflow automation (scheduled scripts, pipelines)
23. Introductory predictive analytics (when to hand off to data science)
24. Risk and insurance analytics: claims analysis, portfolio monitoring, reporting

## Part VI: Applied Work
25. Reconciliation and reporting project (telecom internship)
26. Automation and reporting project (bank internship)
27. Insurance data analysis from the PFE
28. Portfolio of dashboards and case studies

---

## How the two books relate
Parts I and II overlap on purpose, since both roles share the same base. They diverge from the modeling stage on: Data Science goes deeper into models and deployment, while Data Analyst goes deeper into communication and decision support.
